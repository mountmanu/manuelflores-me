# Knowledge base — books to acquire (PDF)

The old folder held 66 summaries of sales-methodology books, retired with the
SE positioning. This one is empty until you drop PDFs in. Below is a proposed
list, organized by what it's actually for. Confidence is marked — some titles
are foundational and certain; a couple of niche ones should be double-checked
(exact author/edition) before you buy.

Suggested filename convention once you add PDFs: `NN-title-slug.pdf`, so a
future skill or script can list them in order.

## Priority 1 — the statistics your own research actually depends on

1. **Peter Christen, *Data Matching: Concepts and Techniques for Record
   Linkage, Entity Resolution, and Duplicate Detection*** (Springer, 2012).
   The standard reference on exactly the problem in your A2 finding —
   matching records across sources when the key is imperfect. High
   confidence this is real and correct.
2. **Bishop, Fienberg & Holland, *Discrete Multivariate Analysis: Theory and
   Practice*** (MIT Press, 1975; reprinted by Springer). The foundational
   text for log-linear models and capture-recapture / multiple systems
   estimation — the method underneath every trafficking prevalence study
   you've reviewed. High confidence.
3. **Amstrup, McDonald & Manly (eds.), *Handbook of Capture-Recapture
   Analysis*** (Princeton University Press, 2005). Applied, worked-example
   companion to #2. High confidence.
4. **Craig K. Enders, *Applied Missing Data Analysis*** (Guilford Press,
   2010). Administrative data is missing data — this is the standard
   applied text. High confidence.

## Priority 2 — research methods for exactly this kind of work

5. **Matthew Salganik, *Bit by Bit: Social Research in the Digital Age***
   (Princeton University Press, 2018). Computational social science with
   found/administrative data — closest existing book to what you're actually
   doing. Free to read online at bitbybitbook.com if you want to check before
   buying a PDF. High confidence.
6. **King, Keohane & Verba, *Designing Social Inquiry: Scientific Inference
   in Qualitative Research*** (Princeton University Press, 1994). The
   standard text on inference and research design; useful for defending your
   method in an interview. High confidence.
7. **Rossi, Lipsey & Henry, *Evaluation: A Systematic Approach*** (Sage —
   now in later editions with Henry as third author). The standard program-
   evaluation textbook; RTI/Urban/Abt-style firms live in this framework.
   High confidence on the book; check current edition/author order.

## Priority 3 — the ethics and politics of measuring vulnerable people

8. **Virginia Eubanks, *Automating Inequality: How High-Tech Tools Profile,
   Police, and Punish the Poor*** (St. Martin's Press, 2018). Directly about
   administrative data systems in child welfare and benefits programs — the
   closest thing to a critique of the exact systems you're measuring. High
   confidence, and probably the single most relevant non-statistics book on
   this list.
9. **Cathy O'Neil, *Weapons of Math Destruction*** (Crown, 2016). Widely
   read; useful shorthand in interviews for the harms of unaudited models.
   High confidence.
10. **Catherine D'Ignazio & Lauren Klein, *Data Feminism*** (MIT Press,
    2020). On whose data gets collected and who it's collected about — for
    interviews with NGOs and mission-driven orgs. High confidence. Free to
    read online at data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu.

## Priority 4 — domain knowledge on trafficking and child welfare

11. **Kevin Bales, *Disposable People: New Slavery in the Global Economy***
    (University of California Press, 1999, updated editions). The
    foundational academic text in modern trafficking studies. High
    confidence.
12. **Kevin Bales & Ron Soodalter, *The Slave Next Door: Human Trafficking
    in America Today*** (University of California Press, 2009). US-focused
    companion to #11. High confidence.
13. **Siddharth Kara, *Sex Trafficking: Inside the Business of Modern
    Slavery*** (Columbia University Press, 2009). Widely cited in the
    field. High confidence.
14. A current edited academic volume on **child welfare systems research** —
    I don't have a specific title I can vouch for with confidence here.
    Rather than hand you a possibly-wrong citation, search Google Scholar
    or a university library catalog for "child welfare administrative data
    research" and pick the most-cited recent edited volume; Chapin Hall
    (University of Chicago) publishes several open-access ones directly on
    their site, free, no PDF-acquisition needed.

## Priority 5 — writing (your actual strongest ground)

15. **Booth, Colomb & Williams, *The Craft of Research*** (University of
    Chicago Press). The standard text on writing up research for a
    non-specialist reader — matches your BA in rhetoric and writing and your
    strongest interview asset. High confidence.
16. **Darrell Huff, *How to Lie with Statistics*** (1954, still in print).
    Short, classic, and a genuinely useful refresher on where prevalence
    estimates go wrong — pairs directly with your A2 finding. High
    confidence.

## Not included, on purpose

Nothing on sales, discovery, demos, MEDDIC, or pitching. That entire shelf is
retired with the SE positioning.
