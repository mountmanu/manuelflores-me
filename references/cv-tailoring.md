# Phase 2 — CV and cover letter

**Read `assets/cv-source.md` in full before writing a single line.** Every
claim in a tailored CV must trace to that file. If a JD wants something that
is not in there, the answer is that he does not have it — say so and work
around it. Do not manufacture a bullet.

## The two variants

There is no third. Pick by what the JD's day-one work actually is.

### Variant R — Research analyst (default)
Order: Summary → Research Projects → Core Competencies → Professional
Experience → Education.

The four artifacts lead because they are the only thing on the page that a
research hiring panel has not seen a hundred times. Areté is compressed to a
single block with two or three bullets, framed as data engineering against
messy third-party inputs.

Use for: research analyst, data analyst, research associate, project data
lead, evaluation roles, anything at RTI / Urban / NORC / Mathematica / Abt /
Child Trends / Chapin Hall, NGO data roles, university project staff.

### Variant E — Data engineer on a mission team
Order: Summary → Professional Experience → Research Projects → Competencies →
Education.

Areté leads because the role is building and maintaining pipelines. The
artifacts become proof he chose this domain rather than landing in it.

Use for: data engineer / data infrastructure roles at Thorn, Polaris, Allies
Against Slavery, or a federal contractor building child-welfare systems.

## The bullet-swap protocol

1. **Extract from the JD** the five or six concrete verbs and nouns —
   "administrative data", "record linkage", "data quality", "reproducible",
   "Stata/R/Python", "reporting to non-technical stakeholders", "Spanish".
2. **Map each to something already true** in `cv-source.md`. Do not stretch.
   The honest mappings that recur:
   - *administrative data* → the AB 388 panel, joined to a licensing file
   - *data quality / validation* → the seven documented bugs and the
     independent second parser
   - *record linkage* → A2, the linkage-error simulation
   - *reproducibility* → seeded simulation, fingerprinted sources, declared
     gaps
   - *stakeholder communication* → six years of BD/PM, plus writing the
     research documents for a non-specialist reader
   - *Spanish* → native, with genuine cross-border sourcing
3. **Rewrite only three to five bullets.** Everything else stays. A CV rebuilt
   from scratch for every posting drifts away from the truth and reads
   generic.
4. **Do not add a skill because the JD names it.** If the JD wants R or Stata
   and he has Python, say Python. Volunteering "familiar with R" when he has
   not used it is exactly the kind of small lie that ends an interview.

## The cover letter

For research roles the cover letter matters more than it did in SE hiring,
because it is the writing sample when none is requested. He has a BA in
rhetoric and writing; this is his strongest ground.

Four paragraphs, one page, no more:

1. **The specific thing about their work.** Not "I admire your mission." Name
   a study, a dataset, a report, and say something substantive about it. For
   any team that has published prevalence work, A2 is the natural opener.
2. **What he built and what it found.** One artifact, concrete, with the
   number. Not all four.
3. **The rigor, shown not claimed.** The independent second parser, or the
   seven documented bugs, or a correction he left in place. One sentence.
   This is the paragraph that separates him from other non-credentialled
   applicants.
4. **The honest ask.** What he wants from the role — usually access to data
   and a team — and an offer to send the code.

Do not write around the missing degree. If it is relevant, one clause
acknowledging it and moving on reads as confidence; silence reads as evasion
when it surfaces later.

## Anti-patterns — refuse these even if asked

- Any sales-methodology vocabulary. MEDDIC, discovery, demo, POC, pipeline in
  the sales sense, "technical win". All retired and actively disqualifying
  here.
- VisionGuard, LoanSight, Embedded Vision Summit, edge AI, YOLOv8, Raspberry
  Pi. Permanently retired.
- "8+ years" of anything technical. Two years of production data work.
- A publications section, or anything implying peer review.
- Contract values, revenue figures, client counts as commercial achievement.
  True, but they signal a vendor, not a researcher.
- Describing the four artifacts as published, peer-reviewed, or commissioned.

## Output format

Give him the changed bullets only, marked old → new, plus which variant and
why. Do not print the whole CV back unless he asks.
