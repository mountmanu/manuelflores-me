# Phase 3 — Outreach

Outreach matters more here than in commercial hiring. Research and
grant-funded posts bend for a named advocate inside, and that is the main
lever Manuel has against the credential gap. See `assets/positioning.md`.

## The one thing that makes his outreach work

He can open with a **real technical question about the recipient's own
published work** — because he has actually read it and found something
nobody addressed. Almost nobody cold-emailing a researcher can do that. It is
also simply the honest thing to send, because the answer is genuinely unknown
to him.

This is why A2 (the linkage-error finding) is the best outreach asset he has,
even though A1 is the more finished piece of work. A2 is a question. A1 is a
result. Questions get replies.

## Types

| Type | When | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Researcher / PI note | He has read their work and has a genuine question | ≤250 words |
| Hiring manager note | A specific posting, no prior contact | ≤150 words |
| Recruiter reply | They contacted him | ≤120 words |
| Follow-up on silence | 10–14 days after, once only | ≤60 words |
| Post-interview note | Within 24 hours | ≤120 words |

## Researcher / PI note — the template that matters

Structure, in order:

1. **What of theirs he read**, named specifically. A study, not a body of work.
2. **The question.** Concrete, technical, answerable, and genuinely open. Not
   rhetorical, not a setup for a pitch.
3. **What he did about it.** One or two sentences with the number. Framed as
   "I built something small to get a feel for the size of the effect", not as
   a finding he is asserting over theirs.
4. **The explicit uncertainty.** What he could not check without their data,
   and an invitation to tell him he is missing something. This is not
   politeness — it is the part that makes a working scientist reply.
5. **A small, concrete offer.** The code, in one line, no attachment.
6. **One sentence on who he is.** Not a résumé.

A live draft of exactly this exists at
`Desktop/op-regnum/anti-trafficking-career/research/NOVEL/OUTREACH-DRAFT-tueller.md`
— addressed to Stephen Tueller at RTI, still unsent, still needing his own
closing line and a verified current address. **Reuse its structure; do not
reuse its text for a different recipient.**

Rules for these:
- **Verify the address** through the organization's own directory before
  sending. Guessing is worse than asking.
- **Never lead with the job.** If the conversation goes well, the role comes
  up on its own or in a second message. Opening with "I am interested in
  opportunities" converts a colleague into a gatekeeper.
- **Do not attach anything.** Offer; let them ask.
- If they reply with "we assumed X because Y", **write it down and treat it as
  a correction to the research**, not an objection to overcome. That is the
  entire point of sending it.

## Hiring manager note

1. The posting, named.
2. One artifact, one number, one sentence — the one closest to what the
   posting actually does.
3. One line on the rigor, concrete: the second independent parser, or the
   documented bugs.
4. The ask: a short conversation, or permission to send the code.

No mission language. No "I am passionate about". They receive a hundred of
those and none from someone who has already done the work unpaid.

## When he pastes a recruiter email

1. Say plainly whether the role passes triage. Do not soften it.
2. If yes: draft the reply, confirm interest, ask the two questions that
   actually matter — what data the role touches, and whether the credential
   listed is a hard requirement.
3. If no: a two-line decline that leaves the door open.

## Never

- Never claim publications, affiliation, peer review or data access he does
  not have.
- Never describe the artifacts as published while they are local. "Built, not
  yet published, happy to send the code" is stronger than a vague claim
  anyway.
- Never send the same note to two people at one organization.
- Never follow up more than once.
- Never use sales vocabulary. These are colleagues, not prospects.
